## Campaign Concept

EGB කෑම කලාව was designed around a simple but operationally demanding idea — guests purchase a can of EGB to gain entry, and inside they experience a full food and entertainment environment built around the product.

The event combined:
- **Live musical performances** — creating a destination event, not just a brand activation
- **20+ food stalls** — each presenting a different style of cuisine or dish paired with EGB, demonstrating the product's versatility across food experiences
- **Interactive games and activities** — keeping attendees engaged throughout the day
- **Product integration at every touchpoint** — EGB was not just present at the event; it was the event

The operational challenge was not the concept — it was making a complex, multi-vendor, high-footfall event run without visible friction, so that the brand experience felt seamless to every attendee.

## Phase 1 — Pre-Campaign (10 Days)

### Objective
Build the full operational foundation for the mega event day — site, vendors, product, logistics, and team all ready to execute without improvisation on the day.

### Site Visits & Venue Preparation
- Conducted site inspections to assess layout, access points, power requirements, traffic flow, and crowd management considerations
- Mapped vendor placement across the site — food stalls, entertainment stage, games areas, entry and exit points — balancing footfall distribution and operational access
- Identified infrastructure requirements: power supply, generator placement, water access, waste management, security perimeter, and signage positions
- Coordinated with local authorities for permits, site approvals, and compliance clearances

### Vendor Sourcing & Briefing
- Sourced and confirmed 20+ vendors across food, entertainment, logistics, fabrication, staffing, security, audio-visual, and support services
- Prepared individual vendor briefing documents covering scope of work, setup schedule, site access, inter-vendor dependencies, and compliance requirements
- Conducted pre-event vendor briefing session — aligning all parties on the campaign concept, site layout, day-of schedule, and escalation contacts
- Confirmed all vendor contracts, agreed rates, payment terms, and delivery schedules in writing

### Product Procurement & Stock Management
- Coordinated procurement of EGB product stock in volumes sufficient for 10,000 to 15,000 attendees — factoring entry requirements, in-event consumption, and food stall integration
- Managed product intake, storage, and inventory logging — ensuring stock was available, accounted for, and correctly allocated across entry points and vendor stalls
- Planned product distribution logistics — timing, quantities per site zone, replenishment cycles during the event, and post-event stock reconciliation
- Monitored product condition and handling standards throughout the pre-campaign period

## Phase 2 — Mega Event Day

### Scale & Complexity
Managing a 10,000 to 15,000-person event with 20+ active vendors simultaneously is a real-time logistics and coordination challenge. The event concept — where product purchase is the entry mechanism — means that product availability, entry flow management, and vendor readiness are all critical path items from the moment gates open.

### Setup & Deployment
- Managed vendor arrival schedule from early morning — each vendor assigned a specific arrival window, site access point, and setup deadline
- Supervised setup across all zones: stage and audio-visual, food stall construction and product loading, games and activity areas, security positioning, signage installation, and entry gate configuration
- Conducted final readiness check across all vendor zones before gates opened — no zone cleared without sign-off
- Managed product distribution to entry points and food stalls — ensuring correct quantities were in place and replenishment logistics were staged and ready

### On-Ground Operations
- Maintained active coordination across all vendor workstreams throughout the event — monitoring performance, managing issues in real time, and making operational decisions as conditions changed
- Managed product replenishment cycles during the event — tracking consumption rates at entry gates and food stalls, coordinating stock movement from central storage to deployment points
- Supervised cross-functional team across logistics, vendor management, crowd flow, and product handling
- Managed vendor dependencies in real time — food stall operations, entertainment schedule, and guest flow were all interdependent and required continuous coordination

### Crowd & Flow Management
- With 10,000 to 15,000 attendees, entry gate management was a critical operational priority — product purchase at entry required sufficient stock, staffing, and processing capacity to prevent bottlenecks
- Site layout decisions made during the pre-campaign phase directly influenced crowd distribution — food stall placement, stage positioning, and games areas were mapped to draw footfall across the full site rather than concentrating it at single points

## Key Operational Challenges

| Challenge | How It Was Managed |
|---|---|
| Product as entry mechanism | Required precise stock forecasting and reliable entry-point replenishment to prevent gate failures |
| 20+ vendor coordination | Master vendor schedule with dependency mapping — each vendor briefed individually and collectively |
| High footfall logistics | Site zoning and footfall distribution planned during pre-campaign to prevent congestion |
| Product integration across 20+ food stalls | Each stall briefed on product handling, presentation, and replenishment requirements |
| Real-time stock management | Replenishment cycles planned in advance with dedicated logistics staff managing movement during the event |
| Multi-vendor dependencies | Critical path mapped pre-event — stage cannot open before AV complete, stalls cannot open before product loaded |

## Operational Learnings

- **Entry mechanism logistics require the same rigour as supply chain operations.** When product purchase is the gate, stock management at entry points is as critical as any warehouse operation — a stockout at the gate stops the event.
- **Pre-campaign investment determines event-day outcomes.** Every operational problem on event day can be traced to something that was not resolved in the preparation phase.
- **Vendor briefing at scale requires structure, not just communication.** With 20+ vendors, verbal briefings are insufficient. Written scope, confirmed schedules, and a shared site map are the minimum standard.
- **Crowd flow is a logistics problem.** How people move through a site — and where they concentrate — is directly shaped by operational decisions about layout, product placement, and access points.
- **Real-time coordination requires decision-making authority.** The operations coordinator must be empowered to make on-the-spot decisions. Escalating every issue delays resolution and compounds problems at scale.

## Relevance to Supply Chain & Operations Management

This campaign required the simultaneous application of core supply chain and operations principles in a live, high-pressure environment:

- **Demand forecasting** — estimating product volumes for 10,000 to 15,000 attendees across multiple consumption points
- **Inventory management** — procurement, storage, allocation, and real-time replenishment of product stock
- **Vendor network management** — sourcing, briefing, coordinating, and evaluating 20+ vendors
- **Logistics coordination** — product movement from procurement through storage to deployment across multiple site zones
- **Operations planning** — 10-day pre-campaign phase structured as a formal project with milestones, dependencies, and sign-off gates
